# Cover art prompts

Most of the library has no artwork, so covers get generated. These prompts are
grouped by the **mood tags the analyser already produces**, so a cover can be
picked to match what the track actually sounds like rather than at random.

Every prompt is written for a square 1:1 cover with **no text** — an AI-rendered
title is almost always misspelled, and the app draws the real title over the
tile anyway.

---

## How to use these

1. Look up a track's moods (Now Playing → MOOD shows them).
2. Take the matching section below.
3. Append one **style** line and one **palette** line from the bottom.
4. Generate 3–4 and keep the one that fits.

Randomising the style and palette per track is what stops a library of
generated covers looking like one repeated image.

**Always append:** `square 1:1 album cover, no text, no lettering, no watermark`

## Style modifiers — pick one per cover

- `90s anime cel animation still, visible cel texture, hand-painted background`
- `modern anime key visual, clean digital linework, soft cinematic lighting`
- `Studio Ghibli inspired painted background, no characters, warm naturalism`
- `flat vector illustration, bold shapes, minimal detail, thick outlines`
- `retro city pop album art, 1980s Japanese aesthetic, halftone print texture`
- `soft watercolour and coloured pencil, visible paper grain, gentle bleed`
- `high-contrast manga panel, screentone shading, dramatic ink blacks`
- `risograph print, two-colour overlay, deliberate misregistration`
- `low-poly 3D render with soft studio lighting, matte pastel materials`
- `chunky pixel art, limited 32-colour palette, dithered gradients`

## Palettes — pick one per cover

- `muted sage green, cream and warm grey`
- `dusty rose, terracotta and soft gold`
- `deep indigo, teal and a single warm amber accent`
- `washed pastel — mint, pale pink, powder blue`
- `high-contrast black, bone white and one vivid red`
- `sodium-orange streetlight against cold blue shadow`
- `sun-bleached 70s film — ochre, olive, faded orange`
- `monochrome blue-grey with one saturated highlight`
- `warm paper cream, soft brown ink, muted teal` *(matches the app's own theme)*

## Notes

**Match the mood, not the lyrics.** These are covers for tracks whose titles the
app often only half-recovered from a filename. A cover that fits the *sound* is
right far more often than one guessing at the subject.

**Vary the composition, not just the palette.** A library where every cover is a
centred figure gets monotonous fast even with different colours. The prompts
above deliberately mix wide landscapes, close crops, empty scenes and figures.

**Square, no text.** Generated lettering is nearly always wrong, and the app
overlays the real title on the tile regardless.

**The app's fallback already works.** Missing artwork is drawn as a tinted tile
keyed to the track's content hash — stable per track, never the same colour
twice in a row on screen. Generated covers are an upgrade, not a repair.
